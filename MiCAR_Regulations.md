This document outlines how the Pi Network plans to achieve legal compliance within the European Union using specific technical ecosystem standards (referred to as PiRC standards).
The compliance matrix maps network architectures against four major European regulatory frameworks:
MiCAR (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation)
GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)
AMLD6 (6th Anti-Money Laundering Directive)
DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)
Detailed Section Breakdown
1. Asset & Utility Classification Mapping (MiCAR)
This section addresses compliance with European crypto-asset market regulations:
Art. 3 (Asset Classification): Uses PiRC-101 and PiRC-900 to restrict tokens strictly to network utility constraints. Real-World Assets (RWA) are bridged and handled separately.
Art. 4-18 (Issuer Obligations & Whitepapers): Employs PiRC-202 and PiRC-215 for automated dynamic disclosures. Contract parameters prevent unauthorized token minting without a utility lockup.
Art. 19 (Conflict of Interest): Uses PiRC-Decentralized-Governance. It relies on Soroban-based voting structures to prevent any single entity from overriding decisions.
2. GDPR & AMLD6 Alignment Layer
This highlighted section focuses on privacy and financial crime regulations:
GDPR Minimalization: Achieved via off-chain IPFS mapping (PiRC-808 Decentralized Identity) paired with zero-knowledge (ZK) hooks to keep personal data off the main ledger.
Right to Be Forgotten: Implements "Regulated Vaults" containing a burn/revocation standard. This allows the clean decoupling of identity associations when a user requests deletion.
AMLD6 Travel Rule: Integrated directly into Regulated Mode Smart Contracts. This setup permits compliant financial institutions to transparently attach mandatory transaction metadata.
3. ESG & DORA Metrics (Sustainability)
This section covers environmental standards and operational resilience:
Consensus Efficiency: Adopts the Stellar/Soroban consensus mechanism to generate only a fraction of the carbon output compared to traditional Proof of Work (PoW) networks.
Tracking: Features built-in compliance trackers to measure exact energy expenditure per 10,000 transactions.
